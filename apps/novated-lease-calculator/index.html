The EV discount, explained

Electric Car Discount (FBT exemption). Battery-electric and hydrogen cars first held and used after 1 July 2022, priced under the luxury car tax threshold for fuel-efficient vehicles ($91,661 for 2026–27), are exempt from fringe benefits tax. On a petrol car you'd normally make a post-tax payment of 20% of the car's price each year to cover FBT — for an EV that disappears, so the whole lease and every running cost comes out of pre-tax salary.

What changes from 1 April 2027

  • Leases committed on or before 31 March 2027 keep the full exemption for the whole lease — existing leases are not affected.
  • From 1 April 2027, EVs priced at $75,000 or less keep the full exemption; EVs over $75,000 (up to the LCT threshold) get a 25% FBT discount instead.
  • From 1 April 2029, all eligible EVs move to the 25% discount.
  • Plug-in hybrids lost the exemption from 1 April 2025, unless the lease was committed before then.
These changes were announced with the 2026–27 Budget and are still subject to legislation. This calculator models the 25% discount as reducing the car's taxable value by 25%.

Victoria

  • There is no per-kilometre EV road-user charge — Victoria's ZEV charge was struck down by the High Court in 2023.
  • Victoria's $3,000 ZEV purchase subsidy closed in 2023; there is no state EV rebate to add on top.
  • Stamp duty and rego still apply — put them in “On-road costs” and “Registration”.

Things to know

  • Reportable fringe benefits. Even when exempt, the EV benefit appears on your income statement. It doesn't change your income tax, but it counts towards the Medicare levy surcharge, HELP repayments, Centrelink, child support and some super thresholds.
  • Balloon at the end. You owe the residual (set by ATO minimums for the term) plus GST when the lease ends — you can pay it, refinance it, or sell the car.
  • Super. Your employer must still pay super on your pre-packaging salary.
  • Tax uses 2026–27 resident rates (15% / 30% / 37% / 45%), the 2% Medicare levy and the low income tax offset. HELP and the Medicare levy surcharge aren't modelled.
  • GST on the car is credited up to the 2026–27 car limit ($69,883, so at most $6,353). GST on charging/fuel, servicing and insurance is also credited; rego carries none.
